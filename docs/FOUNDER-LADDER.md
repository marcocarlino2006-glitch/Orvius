# The founder ladder

What actually separates the founders who build generational companies,
recorded plainly so it can be worked against. Technical skill is not on
this list — it can be hired and, here, is already covered.

## What the top tier does differently

1. **They sell before it's comfortable.** The product is never the
   bottleneck for long; the founder's willingness to be in rooms,
   on calls, and posting publicly is. Distribution is a founder job
   that is never delegated early.
2. **They recruit believers, not employees.** The first three people
   decide the company's ceiling. Each one must be someone who makes
   the founder faster, not someone who needs managing.
3. **They make capital a weapon, timed to proof.** Raise (or don't)
   from evidence, in the sequence that maximizes leverage: proof →
   price of money falls → raise more, cheaper, or never.
4. **They control the narrative.** The market believes a story told
   consistently by the founder in public. Posting is not marketing
   chores; it is how the category gets defined before competitors
   define it.
5. **They decide fast and revisit without ego.** Speed of the
   decide→measure→correct loop, run on the week's worst metric.
6. **They protect the bottleneck.** Their calendar is the strategy.
   Everything that is not the current bottleneck is refused, however
   interesting.

## Where this founder stands (honest, Oct 2026)

- Strengths already visible: product taste (the bar that produced
  this codebase), ambition aimed at the right layer (front door →
  graph → endpoint), persistence through a long pre-launch build.
- The gaps are not technical: zero live proof, no distribution
  motion yet, team of effectively one on the critical path, no
  public narrative, no capital story told to anyone.

## The ladder, by milestone (not by date)

- **Rung 1 — Switch flipper.** Toll-free filed, portal sequence done,
  signup open. Nobody else can do this rung.
- **Rung 2 — First seller.** One proof shop live and measured; the
  founder personally sells shops 2–10 in one metro. Metric: booking
  rate and the second shop signed from the first shop's proof.
- **Rung 3 — Storyteller.** A consistent public cadence: real
  numbers, real calls (shared with permission), the density map
  filling in. Metric: inbound interest that the founder didn't chase.
- **Rung 4 — Recruiter.** Believer #2 (and #3) hired against the
  bottleneck the numbers name — likely support/ops or growth, not
  more building. Metric: the founder off the critical path for one
  full week without the company slowing.
- **Rung 5 — Capital strategist.** With density in one metro and
  payments attach measured, choose: raise to buy the second metro,
  or stay lean and let the rake fund it. Either is defensible; drifting
  into it is not.
- **Rung 6 — Category owner.** The endpoint conversation: platforms
  and incumbents negotiating with the graph instead of competing
  with the app. This rung is reached by rungs 1–5 compounding, never
  by skipping.

## Weekly disciplines

- One metric review (the numbers the admin board already prints).
- One public post built from real evidence, not announcements.
- One conversation with a shop owner, recorded as product input.
- One decision written down with its reason, revisited next week.

## Standing rule

The agent builds, measures, audits and fixes. The founder sells,
recruits, tells the story, and flips the switches only they can flip.
When either side does the other's job, the company slows.
