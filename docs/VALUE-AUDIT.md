# Value audit — 2026-10-18

Questioning everything against one standard: does it create measurable
value a shop owner would pay for, and does it compound toward a monopoly?
Recorded so the verdicts steer future work instead of living in chat.

## Value core (the bet)

- **AI receptionist + booking + text confirmation.** The entire thesis.
  Value exists only if the live booking rate holds; evidence today is
  simulation (63 graded scenarios, 600-caller scale sim), zero live calls.
- **Safety classification.** Never in a sales deck, prevents the worst
  day of the company. Graded in English and Spanish. Underinvested.
- **Proof (outcomes, weekly report, Command brief).** The retention
  mechanism: "10 of 16 requests became jobs." High value, low cost.

## Retention layer (valuable, not differentiating)

- **Command OS (Inbox, Jobs, Schedule, Customers, Team), field/tech
  experience, price book, estimates, invoices.** Parity plumbing.
  Necessary for stickiness, never the reason anyone buys, and it competes
  on the incumbent's home turf. **FREEZE new breadth** — what exists is
  enough; new FSM features only when a live shop demands them.

## Money layer (where the valuation lives)

- **Payments, deposits, financing.** The rake. Deserves more investment
  than any FSM feature. Goal: every booked job touches our rails without
  the owner choosing to. Attach is measured on the admin board.
- **Win-back / reactivation.** Found revenue at near-zero marginal cost,
  differentiated by call data. Candidate for its own outcome fee.

## Monopoly bet (highest ceiling, premature)

- **Orvius Network (PASS/TAKE, 5% fee).** The only structurally multi-b
  feature — converts competitors into inventory. **Keep the seed, spend
  nothing more until one trade×metro cell reaches density** (5+ shops;
  the admin Density card flags it). Feature-complete and market-empty is
  the classic platform trap.
- **Ask bar.** Convenience, zero moat. Polish only.
- **Call gallery / replays.** Growth infrastructure, not a shop feature.
  Judge it in the funnel, not in the workspace.

## Hardest questions (open)

1. **Breadth.** 177 API routes pre-revenue is either a masterpiece or
   over-building; only live shops decide. The first 10 shops need four
   things flawless: answer, book, confirm, prove.
2. **Pricing.** Flat seats cap us at software multiples. Outcome pricing
   (per booked job / share of captured value) is where the multi-b
   economics live. $399 Pro is an anchor, not a conclusion.
3. **Trade scope.** Three trades at 90% depth vs one trade owned
   completely. The market grades depth, not coverage.

## Operating rules this audit sets

- Portal first; after launch, investment follows what the first shops
  actually touch (the 20% that carries willingness to pay).
- Investment priority: booking-rate proof → payments attach → density →
  Network. Everything else is a cost center with good design until a
  live number promotes it.
- The loops are the company: weekly call grading into sim scenarios,
  craft audits of signed-in screens, one worst metric fixed per cycle.
